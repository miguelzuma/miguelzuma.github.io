I work across wave-optics theory, public software and the analysis of real gravitational-wave and cosmological data.

The research programme

Most of my effort goes into one connected problem: what wave-optics lensing can tell us about the dark contents of the Universe.

Wave-optics lensing phenomena

Diffraction and interference imprint the mass distribution of a lens directly on a gravitational-wave signal. We build the theory and the methods that read it back out.

Dark matter and compact structures

Lensing is one of the few probes sensitive to dark matter on sub-galactic scales, where the leading candidates disagree most sharply.

Strong-field and multi-messenger lensing

Black holes are excellent lenses, and a binary is a lens whose caustics move. Combining lensed waves with lensed light constrains the lens far better than either alone.

Tests of gravity and dark energy

A lens breaks the symmetry of the background, letting waves couple to new fields. Birefringence and dispersion become tests of general relativity that need no electromagnetic counterpart.

Methods, inference and software

Fast wave-optics solvers, statistical searches and the public codes GLoW and hi_class — so the results are reproducible by anyone.

GLoW and hi_class

Two public codes maintained for the community: a wave-optics lensing solver, and an Einstein–Boltzmann solver for dark energy and modified gravity.
